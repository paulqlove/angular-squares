You can put a football squares board on your TV by opening the game on a computer, pressing Present, and mirroring that browser tab to the screen.

At most squares parties the board lives on one phone that gets passed around the couch while somebody asks who has the 3 and the 7. There is a TV in the room, and it is much easier to read.

A bar with a spare TV is a good fit, and so is a Super Bowl party with an extra computer monitor. Any screen that nobody is using will do.

QuarterScore's TV mode is free for every account. Here is what it shows, how to get it onto a TV, and a checklist for the day of the game.

## What the TV screen shows

- The 10x10 board, with each player's squares in their own color
- The live score and game clock, when the board is linked to a live game
- The winner of each quarter
- How many squares are claimed and how many are open
- A QR code

The screen resizes to fill whatever it is on. We loaded it at sizes from a 13-inch laptop up to a 4K TV, including a 16-inch laptop screen and a 1080p TV, and the whole board, the score and the QR code fit without scrolling.

The QR code opens the same shareable board link you get from Share. A friend who scans it sees the board on their own phone, like any other player. Nobody needs an account.

## How to get it onto the TV

On a computer or laptop, open your game and press **Present**. The button shows on the game page for the host and co-host.

<img src="/assets/blog/tv-mode/game-present-button.png" alt="The top of a QuarterScore game page, with the Share, Present and Your Games buttons next to the board title." width="1400" height="138">

A spare monitor works the same way as a TV: connect it to the computer and put the page on it. Then pick one way to get that tab onto the screen. These are the standard ways to mirror a browser tab. We have not tested them on every TV, so try yours before game day.

- Chrome or Edge: open the browser menu, choose Cast, pick your TV, and set Sources to Cast tab. The computer and the TV need to be on the same Wi-Fi. Google's help page, [Cast from Chrome to your TV](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/3228332), has the details.
- Safari on a Mac: open Control Center, choose Screen Mirroring, and pick your Apple TV or AirPlay TV.
- Any browser: plug an HDMI cable from the computer into the TV. Or open the page in the TV's own web browser.

There is also a **Copy TV link** button. It gives you a link that opens the board live on any browser or device, with no sign-in. That is handy for the TV's own browser, or for a second laptop in another room.

## If you host more than one board

While you are signed in, open quarterscore.com/present without a board code and TV mode rotates through all of your boards. You pick how often it switches: every 10, 20, 30 or 60 seconds. The left and right arrow keys switch boards by hand. When a board's score changes, the rotation stays on that board for 30 seconds. Finished games are skipped.

This works well for a bar or a big family gathering where several groups each have their own board.

## Game-day checklist

Before kickoff:

1. Open your board on a computer the day before and press Present. Make sure the TV shows it.
2. Check that every square is claimed, or that the room knows which are open. The TV shows both counts.
3. Make sure the board is linked to the live game, so the score and clock appear.
4. Plug the computer in, and put the TV where the whole room can see the QR code.

At kickoff:

5. Press Present and confirm the board is on the TV.
6. Point out the QR code to anyone who wants the board on their phone.
7. Keep the computer awake and on the same Wi-Fi as the TV if you are casting.

At the end of the game:

8. During the fourth quarter the Q4 tile says Q4. When the game is over it says Final. The game page and the TV show the same thing, so the room and the phones agree.
9. Read out the winner of each quarter from the TV.
