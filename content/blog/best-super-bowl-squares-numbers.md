The numbers get drawn, you check your square, and someone across the room laughs. You have a 2 and a 5. A friend has a 7 and a 0 and keeps bringing it up. Is their square really better? A little, yes. NFL scoring history shows that some digits come up far more often than others. It also shows why that edge is small.

## How the scoring works

A squares board is a 10x10 grid. One team runs across the top, the other down the side, and each row and column gets a digit from 0 to 9. When a quarter ends, the square where the two teams' last digits meet wins. A score of 24 to 17 is the 4 and 7 square.

Only the last digit of each score counts. Football points come in chunks of 7 and 3, so some last digits turn up much more than others.

## What the history says

Play Football Squares counted the last digit of team scores across [16,812 NFL games through September 2021](https://playfootballsquares.com/most-common-numbers-in-football-squares/), using Pro Football Reference data. This is how often each digit ended a final score:

| Last digit | How often it ended a final score |
| --- | --- |
| 7 | 19.6% |
| 0 | 19.6% |
| 3 | 13.1% |
| 1 | 10.6% |
| 4 | 8.9% |
| 6 | 8.6% |
| 8 | 6.6% |
| 9 | 4.9% |
| 5 | 4.2% |
| 2 | 3.8% |

7 and 0 each end about one score in five. A touchdown with the extra point is 7, two of them make 14, three make 21. A shutout, or a 10, 20 or 30, ends in 0. A field goal gives you 3, 13 and 23, which is why 3 comes in third.

4 is middling on its own, just behind 1. It matters because 14, 24 and 34 are common totals, so 4 often shows up next to a 7.

Your square is a pair of digits, so the pairs are what count. [Super Bowl Squares](https://www.superbowlsquares.org/odds) counted every final score in NFL history, 18,235 games from 1920 through the 2025 season. The digit table above stops at 2021, so the two data sets don't line up exactly. In this one, the squares that hit most often are:

1. 0-7 and 7-0: 3.91% each
2. 4-7 and 7-4: 3.33% each
3. 0-0: 2.96%
4. 0-3 and 3-0: 2.90% each
5. 0-4 and 4-0: 2.57% each

Mirror pairs like 0-7 and 7-0 are two different squares on a board, so each gets that rate. All five are built from 0, 7, 3 and 4. If all 100 squares were equally likely, each would hit 1% of the time.

At the other end, the bottom of the digit table is 2 (3.8%), 5 (4.2%) and 9 (4.9%). There aren't many common ways to end on them. A 2 usually takes a safety or a missed extra point, and final scores like 12, 22 or 25 are rare.

Pairs of them are rarer still. In the Super Bowl Squares table, 2-2 hit 0.04% of the time, 5-5 hit 0.15% and 9-9 hit 0.18%.

These are numbers from all NFL games. There have only been about 60 Super Bowls, too few for a table of their own to tell you much, so the bigger sample is the better guide.

## Why it still comes down to the draw

Nobody picks their numbers. On a fair board, everyone claims squares first and the digits are assigned to the rows and columns at random afterward. The 0-7 square can land on someone who has never watched a game, and 2-2 can land on the person who just read this article.

A weak pair isn't dead, either:

- History describes what happened over thousands of games. It doesn't say what tonight's game will do. One safety or one missed extra point and a 2 is in play.
- Even the best square ends a game less than 4% of the time.
- A board with a winner every quarter gets a few more chances for a surprise.
