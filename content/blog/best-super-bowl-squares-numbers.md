You just got handed your square, you look at your two numbers, and someone across the room laughs. Maybe you're holding a 7 and a 0 and feeling great. Maybe you've got a 2 and a 5 and everyone's already feeling sorry for you. So is anyone actually right? Here's what NFL scoring history says about the best Super Bowl squares numbers, and why it still comes down to the luck of the draw.

## Quick refresher: how squares works

A squares board is a 10x10 grid. One team goes across the top, the other goes down the side, and each row and column gets a digit from 0 to 9. Your square is the meeting point of two digits. If the last digit of each team's score matches your square at the end of a quarter (or at the final whistle), that square takes it. A 24-17 score? That's the 4 and 7 square.

So the only thing that matters is the **last digit** of each score. And because football points come in chunks of 7 and 3, some last digits show up way more often than others.

## The strong digits: 0, 7, 3 and 4

The folks at Play Football Squares counted the final-score last digits of [16,812 NFL games through September 2021, using Pro Football Reference data](https://playfootballsquares.com/most-common-numbers-in-football-squares/). Here's how often each digit ended a team's final score:

| Last digit | How often it ended a final score |
| --- | --- |
| 7 | 19.6% |
| 0 | 19.6% |
| 3 | 13.1% |
| 1 | 10.6% |
| 4 | 8.9% |
| 6 | 8.6% |
| 8 | 6.6% |
| 9 | 4.9% |
| 5 | 4.2% |
| 2 | 3.8% |

**7 and 0** are the heavy hitters, ending about one in five scores each. That makes sense: a touchdown plus the extra point is 7, two of them is 14, three is 21, and a shutout (or a 10, 20 or 30) lands on 0.

**3** is the field goal digit. Three points, 13, 23 (a few touchdowns plus a field goal) all end there.

**4** is the sneaky one. On its own it sits around the middle of the pack, just behind 1. But it's great *in a pair*, because 14, 24 and 34 are such common touchdown-heavy totals, and it pairs up with 7 all the time. [Super Bowl Squares' analysis of 18,235 NFL games from 1920 through the 2025 season](https://www.superbowlsquares.org/odds) ranks the best final-score squares like this:

1. 0-7 or 7-0: 3.91%
2. 4-7 or 7-4: 3.33%
3. 0-0: 2.96%
4. 0-3 or 3-0: 2.90%
5. 0-4 or 4-0: 2.57%

Every one of the top five is built from 0, 7, 3 and 4. For comparison, if every square were equal, each would come up 1% of the time.

What about the Super Bowl itself? It's a much smaller sample, but [The Athletic's look at Super Bowl final scores](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6110152/2025/02/04/super-bowl-squares-history-trends-data/) found 7 was the most common ending digit, with 0 and 1 tied right behind it, and 4 and 3 next. Same story, a little shuffled.

## The weak digits: 2, 5 and 9

Down at the bottom of the pile are **2 (3.8%), 5 (4.2%) and 9 (4.9%)** in the [Play Football Squares data](https://playfootballsquares.com/most-common-numbers-in-football-squares/). There aren't many common ways to get there. A 2 usually needs a safety or a missed extra point somewhere, and scores like 12, 22 or 25 just don't happen much.

The worst squares are pairs of these. In the [Super Bowl Squares table](https://www.superbowlsquares.org/odds), 2-2 hit just 0.04% of the time across all those games, 5-5 came in at 0.15% and 9-9 at 0.18%. In The Athletic's Super Bowl count, 2 was the least common ending digit too.

So yes, if you drew 2 and 5, your friends' sympathy is earned. But don't toss your square in the trash yet.

## So why is it still the luck of the draw?

Here's the thing every good host knows: **nobody gets to pick their numbers.** On a fair board, people claim squares first, and the digits get randomly assigned to the rows and columns afterward. The 0-7 square could land on your aunt who's never watched a game, and the 2-2 square could land on the friend who's been studying this exact article.

A few more reasons the "bad" squares still matter:

- **History isn't a promise.** Those percentages describe what *has* happened over thousands of games. They don't tell you what this year's game will do. One weird safety or missed extra point and suddenly a 2 or a 5 is right in play.
- **One game is a tiny sample.** Even the best square comes up less than 4% of the time at the final. Most nights, the winning square is one nobody was hyping.
- **Quarters shake things up.** Scores early in a game are lower, so the mix of last digits changes from quarter to quarter. Plenty of boards crown a winner after every quarter, which means four chances for something unexpected.
- **It's a party, not a math test.** The fun is in the groans and cheers when a late field goal flips the board. The underdog square hitting is half the story you'll be telling next year.

## How to use this at your party

Knowing the numbers is great for game-day chatter. Pull it up when someone's gloating about their 7, or when the 5 holder needs a little hope. Just keep the draw random so every guest gets the same fair shot, and let the scoreboard do the rest.
